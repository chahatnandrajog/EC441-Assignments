# Week 3 — Physical Layer: Guided Media & Digital Signaling

## Overview
This artifact focuses on the physical layer of networking, specifically how data is transmitted over real-world media and how factors like attenuation, noise, and bandwidth impact communication.

Instead of just defining concepts, this report explains how they connect together and why they matter in actual networking systems. The goal was to understand the tradeoffs between signal strength, data rate, and reliability in a more intuitive way.

---

## Key Concepts Covered
- Guided transmission media (twisted pair, coaxial, optical fiber)
- Signal attenuation and link budget calculations
- Bandwidth vs data rate (Shannon capacity)
- Noise and the AWGN channel model
- Digital signaling (unipolar vs bipolar)
- Matched filter receivers
- Bit error rate and the waterfall effect

---

## Takeaways
One of the main takeaways from this artifact is that networking performance is heavily constrained by physical limits. You cannot increase data rate without either increasing bandwidth or improving signal quality.

Another important idea is that small improvements in signal quality can lead to major reductions in error rate, which explains why systems are designed to operate above certain SNR thresholds.

Overall, this artifact helped connect theoretical formulas to real-world networking behavior, especially in systems like Ethernet, fiber networks, and wireless communication.
