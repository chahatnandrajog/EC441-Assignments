# Week 12 – Practice Midterm 2

## Overview
For this week’s artifact, I created a full-length practice midterm exam that mirrors the structure and difficulty of our actual exams. The goal was to simulate real testing conditions while also reinforcing the major concepts from the second half of the course. Along with the exam, I included a complete solutions packet and a condensed study guide organized by lecture topics.

## Topics Covered
This artifact focuses on core material from Lectures 13–20, including:

- Network Layer: forwarding vs routing, data plane vs control plane  
- IP Addressing: CIDR notation, subnetting, and address structure  
- Routing Algorithms: Dijkstra (link-state) and Bellman-Ford (distance vector)  
- IPv4 and NAT: packet structure, TTL, and address translation  
- Transport Layer: UDP vs TCP, multiplexing/demultiplexing  
- Reliable Data Transfer: Stop-and-Wait, Go-Back-N, Selective Repeat  
- TCP Fundamentals: three-way handshake, sequencing, acknowledgments  
- TCP Congestion Control: slow start, AIMD, cwnd vs rwnd

## Approach and Design Choices
I structured the exam to reflect the way questions are asked in class, especially focusing on:
- Conceptual understanding (e.g., forwarding vs routing, TCP behavior)
- Computation-based problems (e.g., subnetting, shortest paths)
- Real-world protocol behavior (e.g., congestion control decisions)

The study guide was organized by lecture to make it easier to review material in the same way it was taught. Each section highlights key ideas rather than just listing formulas, which makes it more useful for understanding and not just memorization.

## AI Usage
I used AI as a tool to help generate and refine this artifact, but not as a replacement for understanding the material.

Specifically, I used AI to:
- Draft exam-style questions aligned with lecture topics  
- Format the exam and solutions in a clean, structured layout  
- Organize the study guide into clearly defined sections  

I then reviewed and adjusted the content to ensure it matched what we covered in class and reflected the level of difficulty expected on exams. This process helped reinforce my understanding of the material while also producing a polished and useful study resource.

## Reflection
Creating a full practice exam forced me to think about the material from a different perspective. Instead of just solving problems, I had to understand how to construct them, which made gaps in my understanding more obvious. The study guide also helped consolidate key ideas across lectures, especially the connections between routing, transport protocols, and congestion control.

Overall, this artifact serves both as a review tool and as a way to demonstrate a deeper level of engagement with the course material.
